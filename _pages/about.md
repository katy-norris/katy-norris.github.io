Welcome! I am a final-year PhD Candidate in the Department of Political Science at University College London. My research primarily focuses on the intersection between politics and education. 

My dissertation examines the politics of higher education, seeking to understand what motivates politicians to expand and restrict access to higher education. I use both quantitative and qualitative methods, drawing on insights from original archival work across universities in the U.S. I find that, amidst the context of the Cold War, the presence of intense geostrategic competition led some states to begin expanding access to higher education to develop advanced technologies to compete with rivals while also training workers to operate these technologies. 

Beyond my dissertation, I am involved in several research projects. These projects explore what motivates politicians to implement reforms to improve education quality, how teacher unions mobilize women to participate in politics, and how the experience of war shapes the quality of democratic institutions. You can find more information about my research projects below.
   
Before joining UCL, I worked in education policy and international development. I also completed an MPP in Inequality and Social Policy from the University of California, San Diego and an MSc in Development Studies from the London School of Economics & Political Science.
